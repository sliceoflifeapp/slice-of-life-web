Three other arrangements exist and are worth knowing about, though they suit a minority of vloggers. A dedicated freelance editor on an ongoing informal basis learns your channel, but you are then managing someone, including the weeks they are away. A production agency is built for campaigns rather than a publishing rhythm, and scoping and approvals cost days before anyone opens a timeline. An in-house editor only makes sense at real volume, and is an employment relationship with everything that carries.

Common questions

How much does it cost to get a vlog edited?

It depends far more on how you buy it than on the video itself. Per-video freelance work is priced per video, commonly $250 to $600 for a vlog, so a long edit with a lot of footage costs more than a short one. Monthly services are priced against a set number of videos a month, so the per-video figure only makes sense once you are actually publishing that often. Ours is a monthly plan starting at $595 for one video a month, and the first video is free. Whoever you use, the two things that move the number most are the finished length and how much raw footage you send.

How long should it take to get an edited vlog back?

A dedicated editor or a monthly service will usually commit to a turnaround, often one to a few business days. Per-video freelance work runs to whatever that person's week looks like, which is fine for a one-off and hard to build a publishing schedule on. Agencies work in weeks because of scoping and approvals, and the date tends to move. Ours is 24 hours from upload, or the end of the next business day for footage that lands at a weekend.

Do I lose creative control if someone else edits my vlog?

Some, and it is worth being honest that this is the real trade rather than the money. You keep the decisions that matter most — what you filmed, what you said, what the video is about — and you give up frame-level choices. If you have an opinion about every cut, you want to be in the timeline yourself or directing an editor directly, and no arrangement on this page will feel right.

What do I need to send an editor?

The raw footage, exactly as it came off the card, and any notes on what the video is about. You should not have to rename files, organise folders or pre-trim anything — if you are being asked to, that work has been pushed onto you. Most arrangements set up a shared folder once and you drop the day's footage into it after that.

Can I try an editing service before committing?

You should insist on it. Ask for one video cut from your own footage before any money changes hands — a portfolio shows you what someone can do with ideal material, not what they will do with yours. We do this as standard for anyone we take on.
