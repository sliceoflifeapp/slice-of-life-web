Best AI vlog editors for Mac (2026)

We make one of the tools on this list, so read it with that in mind. We have tried to be straight about what each one is for — including where ours is the wrong choice.

Last updated August 2026 · Written by the team behind Slice of Life

“AI video editor” covers at least four different jobs, which is why comparing them is confusing. Some tools clip long video into shorts. Some strip out silence. Some are professional timelines with AI features added. Some are broad suites that do a bit of everything.

Only a couple take a pile of raw footage and turn it into a watchable video — which is the actual problem if you film yourself talking to camera and never get around to posting.

The tools

Slice of Life

Best for: If you talk to camera and the assembling is what stops you posting.

You drop in a day of raw footage. It finds your talking-to-camera moments, works out which of your own b-roll matches what you are saying, cuts it in, and gives you a finished video. You adjust the whole arrangement in a review screen first — reorder, cut words, swap clips, change pacing — so you are correcting a draft instead of starting from an empty timeline. Everything runs on your Mac, and it exports an editable timeline if you want to finish in a real NLE.

The catch: It only does one kind of video. If you are not talking to camera, most of what it does has nothing to work with.

Descript

Best for: If you want one tool covering video, podcasts and transcripts.

The broadest tool on this list. Its signature idea is editing by transcript — delete a sentence and the video goes with it — and it is genuinely excellent at that for talking-head work. Around it sits screen recording, podcast production, AI voice, collaboration and captions. If your work spans more than vlogs, that breadth is the reason to pick it, and it is a mature product with a real support team behind it.

The catch: It makes editing faster; it does not decide what the video should be. You still build the shape yourself and place every piece of b-roll. Your media is uploaded to the cloud, and it is a lot of surface area if all you make is vlogs.

CapCut

Best for: Clipping, captions and short-form.

Most people reach for CapCut to cut something down and get captions on it fast, and it is very good at that. Enormous effects and template library, and it works on desktop and mobile.

The catch: It is a manual timeline with no opinion about what you are making. It will not assemble a long-form vlog for you.

Opus Clip

Best for: Turning a long video into shorts.

Point it at a finished long video and it picks the moments most likely to work as shorts, then crops and captions them.

The catch: It needs a finished video to start from — which is the thing you do not have yet.

TimeBolt and Recut

Best for: Stripping dead air out of long recordings.

Both do one thing well: detect silence and filler, cut it out, and hand the result to your real editor.

The catch: They shorten a recording; they do not build a video. No b-roll, no structure, no finished export.

Final Cut Pro, Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve

Best for: If you like editing, or you need real control.

The professional tools. Nothing here replaces them, and Resolve in particular is extraordinarily capable.

The catch: They do exactly what you tell them and nothing more. For a daily vlogger that is hours per video, every video.

iMovie

Best for: Starting out, and it is already on your Mac.

Simple, already on your Mac, and fine for straightforward cuts.

The catch: You will outgrow it, and it has essentially no automation.

How we would choose

  • You talk to camera and never post because editing takes too long. Us. That specific problem is the whole reason it exists.
  • You want one tool for video, podcasts and transcripts. Descript. Broader than us by a wide margin, and the better buy if your work is not just vlogs.
  • You enjoy editing and want to get better at it. Final Cut or Resolve. No automation makes you a better editor.
  • You mainly need clips and captions. CapCut.
  • You have long videos and want shorts. Opus Clip.
  • You just want the silence gone. TimeBolt or Recut.
  • You are on Windows. Not us — we are Mac-only. Descript, CapCut or Resolve.

Where Slice of Life is the wrong pick

Worth saying plainly, because we would rather you not waste your time:

  • You do not talk to camera. The whole thing is built around finding your narration and cutting b-roll under it. Music-only or pure b-roll footage gives it nothing to work with.
  • You are not on an Apple Silicon Mac. There is no Windows or Intel version.
  • You want AI-generated video. Nothing here is invented — it only edits footage you actually shot.
  • You want to place every cut yourself. You can reorder, cut words, swap clips and change pacing, but if you want frame-level control you want an NLE, and you should use one.
  • You need more than a vlog editor. If you also record podcasts or screen captures, one broader tool will serve you better than us plus three other things.
